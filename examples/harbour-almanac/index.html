    Reading a sounding

    A charted sounding is the depth below chart datum, not the depth you will find. Chart datum here is Lowest Astronomical Tide — the lowest the water is predicted to fall under any combination of sun and moon — so the sounding is close to the worst case and the tide is almost always adding to it.

    The saving is real but it is not yours to keep. Barometric pressure, a long fetch of onshore wind, and river discharge after rain all move the surface away from prediction, and a strong high over the coast can hold the water down by a third of a metre for days.

    Demonstration document. Cape Ansell and the Hollis Reach are invented, and no vessel should be navigated by this page. The methods are real. Tides are a six-constituent harmonic synthesis (M2, S2, N2, K1, O1, M4) at the standard constituent speeds. Sun times use the NOAA solar-position algorithm and are correct for the position given. The moon uses a mean synodic month, so its age can be out by up to about half a day, and no lunar rise or set is offered because that calculation is not attempted here. The spring-neap cycle is derived from the moon rather than modelled separately, so the page cannot print a neap range beside a full moon.
