If your line is lead, galvanized, or unknown

Before your block is trenched

Treat an unknown line as if it were lead until it is verified. Use a filter certified to NSF/ANSI 53 for lead reduction on every tap used for drinking, cooking, and infant formula, and change the cartridge on the schedule the manufacturer prints — an expired cartridge can release what it has captured.

Never draw cooking or formula water from the hot tap. Hot water pulls more lead out of pipe and solder than cold does, and boiling concentrates it rather than removing it.

After the water has sat

Lead enters water while it stands still in the pipe. If a tap has gone unused for more than six hours, run it cold for 30 seconds to two minutes before drawing water to drink — long enough for the water that sat in your service line to clear.

After any work on the main in your street, run the cold taps for five minutes and then clean the screen at the end of each faucet. Work on a main shakes loose scale that has been sitting inside the pipe for decades, and the screens catch it.

Testing and cost

The Water Division tests tap water for lead at no charge for any address in this inventory. Request a kit at the Ward Seven field office or by telephone at 555 0142.

Replacement of the utility-owned portion of the line carries no charge to the property owner. There is no charge for the private-side portion either; the Division replaces the full line in one trench, because a partial replacement can raise lead levels for months afterward.

How each line was classified

Field verification — excavation
The line was exposed at the curb box or main and identified visually.
Field verification — interior meter
The line was identified where it enters the building, by scratch test and magnet.
Records review — tap card
Classified from the original tap card filed when the connection was made.
Records review — plumbing permit
Classified from a permit recording a full replacement of the line after 1986.
Not yet verified
No record survives and the line has not been exposed. Carried as unknown, not as non-lead.

Statistical prediction was not used. Where the Division could not establish a material from a record or an excavation, the connection is carried as unknown and is scheduled for field verification. This is slower than modelling and produces a larger unknown count than neighbouring systems report; the Division takes the position that a household is owed a fact about its own pipe rather than a probability.
