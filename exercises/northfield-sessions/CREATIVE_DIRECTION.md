# Creative Direction — Northfield Sessions (independent music festival)

Practical Exercise 4 (Expressive marketing website), built under the revised process.

## Product Brief

- **What it does**: the marketing/info page for a 3-day independent music festival — needs to feel exciting and worth traveling for, while still doing the real job (lineup, dates, ticket tiers, venue/logistics).
- **Primary users**: prospective attendees deciding whether to buy a ticket, and ticket-holders checking set times/logistics closer to the date.
- **Primary task**: get excited, then find the specific fact needed (which day an act plays, ticket price, venue address).
- **The asset constraint that shapes this whole direction**: this is a fictional festival with no real photography, no real lineup poster art, no real artist promo shots. Per §3, none of that will be faked. **Real reference anchor for why this isn't fatal to "expressive"**: the Swiss/International Typographic Style poster tradition (`MINIMALISM_RESEARCH.md` Part 1, Vignelli) and Bloomberg Businessweek under Richard Turley (`PROFESSIONAL_WEB_DESIGN_RESEARCH.md` — Neue Hass Grotesk + Publico, "the best consistent set of covers in 40 years," built on bold editorial typography and color, not photography) are both real, well-documented, genuinely expressive visual traditions built primarily on type, color, and composition. A gig-poster/festival-marketing register has real historical precedent for being typography-led rather than photography-led — this is not a workaround invented to dodge the asset gap, it's the actual honest register available.

## No Recipe Without Reason

The obvious template: a full-bleed crowd/stage photo hero with a gradient overlay and a countdown timer — recognizable "festival site" convention. Rejected outright, not stylistically but factually: there is no real photo to use, and faking one (stock crowd photography, an AI-generated "festival" image) is exactly what §3's MUST-NOT list forbids. This isn't a taste preference toward minimalism; it's the asset constraint directly determining the structural direction, the same way it did in the Fenwick Museum exercise.

## Direction (one direction built directly, with the rejected default named above serving as the real alternative that was considered and ruled out — per §5's proportionality guidance, a single-page marketing site with a hard asset constraint doesn't need the full 3-direction exercise machinery to reach a well-reasoned choice)

**"Bill-as-poster"**: the festival's own lineup — band names, stage, day — becomes the literal hero typography, arranged like a real gig-poster bill (headliners largest, support acts smaller, arranged by day) rather than illustrated with any image at all. Color and scale do the work a photo normally would. A single accent color is reserved for one meaning only (which day is selected/active), following Ramp's Bakken & Bæck-anchored single-accent-for-one-semantic-role discipline already used successfully in the Ridgeline exercise — reused here with reason restated, not assumed.

## Typography Decision

- **Display (the bill itself)**: **Archivo Black** / **Archivo** — a real, confident, genuinely poster-capable grotesque (SIL OFL), distinct from every display face used in prior exercises (Big Shoulders Text, Lora). Chosen because the type scale needs enormous range (a headliner name at poster scale down to a stage-time label) while staying one coherent family, matching the Swiss-poster anchor's own logic of typography carrying the entire visual argument.
- **UI/logistics (dates, prices, venue info)**: same family, lighter weight — deliberately not a second typeface, since the "bill as the entire visual system" concept is undermined by introducing a second voice for logistics; Ridgeline and Fenwick both used a two-register split for a *stated reason* (data vs. prose), and this product doesn't have that same content-type split — its logistics content is short enough to stay in the same family without confusion.

## Color Direction

Near-black background (a poster printed on dark stock is a real historical convention, not a reflexively "serious/dark" choice — stated reason: it makes the typographic color-blocking the accent system depends on actually read with full contrast, the same reasoning Direction D used in the dispatch-console exercise, restated for a different product). One saturated accent (a warm magenta-red, chosen to avoid this project's own flagged purple/teal defaults while still reading as energetic) marks the active/selected day only — every other piece of type is white or gray, so the accent stays meaningful per §9's reserve-the-accent rule.

## Asset Strategy

No photography, no illustration, no artist images — classified **not required**, because the selected direction doesn't depend on them (unlike the rejected default). Logo: plain wordmark only.
