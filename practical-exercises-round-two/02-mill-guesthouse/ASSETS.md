# Assets — 02 Bellman's Mill

## What is real and what is not

**The photographs are real and are of a real building: New Abbey Corn Mill, New Abbey, Dumfries and
Galloway.** It is a working eighteenth-century meal mill open to the public. It is *not* a guesthouse
and has no connection to this exercise.

**Bellman's Mill is invented.** The proprietors, the four rooms, the rates, the milling days, the
availability and the enquiry form are written for a design exercise. The village, the hill, the abbey,
the loch and the Solway are real, and the daylight figures are computed for the real latitude, because
a site about when to visit a place is worthless if the place's own facts are made up.

Every photograph is credited in the page beside the image it belongs to, not only here.

### Processing

Originals are 1.9–5.5 Mpx. Each was cropped only to trim dead edge (road, blank sky), resized, and
saved progressive JPEG at the lowest quality that stays under 300 KB at the large size and 110 KB at
the small. Foliage-heavy frames were given fewer pixels rather than a lower quality floor, because
blocking in leaves is more visible than softness. No colour grading, no filters, no compositing: the
place looks like this.

### What was deliberately not done

No photograph was substituted for one that does not exist. There is **no guest-bedroom photography in
this set, and none was faked** — no period-house interior standing in for a room, no stock, no
gradient block. That absence is the single most important asset fact in this exercise and it changed
the design: the rooms are described by a drawn section, measured facts and a photograph of what each
one looks *out at*. See the README.

## Computed, not invented

Daylight hours in the year table are computed for 54.978 °N, 3.617 °W (New Abbey) with the standard
NOAA solar-position algorithm, sunrise to sunset at the 90.833° zenith. Nothing rounds them into a
prettier curve.
