Four rooms, and where they are in the building

A mill is a stack of working floors, so the rooms are stacked too. Which floor a room is on decides its stairs, its noise, its warmth and its view — more than anything else about it does.

The mill's year

Choosing a month here is not the same as choosing a month at a hotel. The lade decides whether the wheel turns, and the latitude decides how much of the day you get.

Daylight is sunrise to sunset on the 15th, computed for 54.978 °N. Rates are the lowest room; each room carries its own on top of the same seasonal band.

Bellman's Mill is invented for a design exercise. The proprietors, the rooms, the rates and the availability do not exist, and the enquiry form sends nothing anywhere. The village, the hill and the abbey are real, and the daylight figures are computed for the real latitude rather than made up.

The photographs are real and are of New Abbey Corn Mill, a working meal mill open to the public, which is not a guesthouse and has no connection to this exercise.
