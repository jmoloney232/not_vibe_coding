A · Core log — Newsreader 19/1.62, 62ch · apparatus in Barlow Semi Condensed · two families, clear division of labour

Timescales · Issue 14

Counting summers by eye

At the top of a deep core the years are thick enough to see, and the count becomes the clock rather than a reading taken from one.

The WAIS Divide core reached bedrock at 3,405 metres in December 2011. Through its upper 577 metres a single year occupies roughly 22 centimetres of ice — thick enough that a technician working under polarised light can mark summer layers by hand, and thick enough that the layer count itself becomes the timescale.

Below about 1,800 metres the layers thin past the point where the eye is any use, and the chronology passes to electrical conductivity and to the gas record. The handover is not seamless, and the published age model carries two different kinds of uncertainty on either side of it.
