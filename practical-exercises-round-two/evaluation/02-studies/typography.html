Bellman's Mill — three typography directions

Identical content in all three: the room page for the Bin Floor. The test is whether the pair can carry (a) a name, (b) 180 words of a proprietor's voice, (c) a fact list where the numbers are the point, and (d) a photograph credit at 12px. Ground, colour and layout are held constant.

Direction A — Literata + Schibsted Grotesk

Reading serif for the voice, grotesque for every measured thing. The conventional hospitality arrangement, executed on a screen serif rather than a display serif.

Room four · top floor

The Bin Floor

Thirty-one steps up, and the only room in the mill you can see Criffel from.

This was where the grain waited. It is the top of the building, under the collar beams, and the sack hoist trap is still in the floor — boarded over, but you will notice it. The window faces south-west across the pond.

We will be honest about the stairs. They are mill stairs: steep, open-tread, turning twice, with a rope rather than a handrail on the last flight. There is nowhere to put a lift and we are not going to pretend otherwise. If that is a problem, take the Wheel Room, which is on the level with the front door.

Direction B — Schibsted Grotesk throughout

One grotesque, hierarchy from size, weight and case. Argues that the mill is a working building rather than a romantic one, and refuses the category's serif reflex.

Direction C — Zilla Slab display + Literata reading

Slab for the name and the apparatus, screen serif for the prose. Aims at a machine-age register — cast-iron gearing, stencilled sacks — rather than a hospitality one.
