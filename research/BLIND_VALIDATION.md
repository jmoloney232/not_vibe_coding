# Blind Validation: Original vs. Redesigned Perigee Hero

Per Part 13: a fresh reviewer (no prior context, not one of the two reviewers used in `INDEPENDENT_REVIEWS.md`) was shown the round-5 (pre-redesign) and round-6 (redesigned) desktop screenshots, unlabeled as "before"/"after," and asked to compare. Full verbatim response below.

## Verbatim reviewer response

> **1. What each page is about, and the main difference:** Both are the same landing page for "Perigee," an orbital-manufacturing startup... The difference is concentrated in one spot: the hero-right visual and one added disclosure line. Version A has a decorative orbit diagram — an ellipse with two dots... Version B replaces that with a labeled bar chart, "GROUND VS. ORBIT-DRAWN MATERIAL"... Version B also adds a paragraph... stating "Perigee is pre-flight."
>
> **2. Which feels more authored:** Version B. The bar chart is a specific decision that ties directly to the two paragraphs already sitting below it in "Why orbit"... Version A's orbiting-dot widget is generic space-startup furniture — it looks the same whether the company is Perigee or any other space company, and it isn't wired to the content around it.
>
> **3. What reads as an "AI-generated interface" cliché:** Version A's orbit diagram: a faint ellipse with two dots and a "velocity/altitude" readout. This is the reflexive default for anything labeled "space"... Version B has no equivalent cliché element; its chart is the fix for that exact problem.
>
> **4. Which is more credible:** Version B... Version A's orbit widget, with specific-sounding numbers (349 km, 7.71 km/s) and only a small "for display" caption, sits closer to the line of looking like live telemetry from flown hardware — riskier for a company that hasn't flown yet.
>
> **5. Remaining weaknesses in each:** Version A: the decorative orbit graphic communicates nothing verifiable... Version B: the "illustrative index" chart, while more honest, is still thin — two metrics, suspiciously clean numbers (6, 9), presented with just enough hedging to be defensible but not enough specificity to be a real proof point... Both share the same tells regardless of which is stronger: the contact address is `engineering@perigee.example` (a reserved placeholder TLD, not a real domain), "PROGRAM 01" numbering with no visible other programs, and a generically confident tagline structure common to deep-tech landing pages. Neither version resolves those.
>
> **6. Which came first:** Version A first, Version B as the revision... That's the signature of someone reviewing A, getting the objection "this looks like it's overselling flight status" or "the hero graphic doesn't say anything," and patching precisely that — not a ground-up redesign.

## Assessment of this result

**What the redesign achieved, per independent confirmation:** the reviewer, with no priming, named the same failure the two round-5 blind reviewers found (a decorative, content-free orbit widget reading as a generic space-startup cliché) and confirmed the replacement chart fixes it *for the specific reason it needed fixing* — because it's now "wired to the content around it," not because it's merely a different decoration. The reviewer also independently surfaced a real risk in the original that neither prior reviewer explicitly named: the illustrative telemetry numbers, without the new stage-honesty disclosure, "sit closer to the line of looking like live telemetry from flown hardware" for a company that hasn't flown — i.e., the original wasn't just generic, it was mildly misleading about the company's actual maturity, and the new stage-note fixes a real, previously-uncaught realism gap, not just an aesthetic one.

**What the redesign did not fix, honestly reported rather than omitted:** the reviewer correctly flagged that the new chart's numbers are "suspiciously clean" and that the chart itself is still "directionally true marketing chart" more than hard data — a fair critique, since the numbers are explicitly illustrative by design (this is fictional content; real flight data cannot be fabricated per the Realism Audit's own rule against fabricated claims) but the reviewer's skepticism is the correct reaction to any illustrative-but-clean-looking number, and is logged here rather than treated as resolved. The reviewer also flagged two things this redesign didn't touch at all: the `.example` placeholder email domain (a deliberate artifact of this being a fictional demo, not a real company — an actual company would of course use a real domain, but this is a genuine, disclosed limitation of doing an exercise on invented content rather than a real product) and the "PROGRAM 01" numbering implying other programs that are never shown or mentioned again — a real, minor, previously-uncaught inconsistency worth fixing in any further iteration.

**Correctly reconstructed the edit history from the artifact alone** — a good sign that the change reads as a "surgical, additive edit on top of an otherwise identical page" responding to a specific objection, not a cosmetic reshuffle, which matches what the redesign plan (`REDESIGN_EXERCISE.md`) actually set out to do (a targeted Tier 6 fix, not a full rebuild).

**Net conclusion, stated with the calibrated confidence this document's own Uncertainty Requirement asks for:** this is real, convergent evidence (three independent reviewers across two rounds, one of whom directly compared both versions) that replacing decorative-but-empty technical framing with genuinely informative content measurably improves both perceived authorship and credibility for this specific instance. It is not evidence that this fix generalizes to all "technical-looking decoration" — `TAXONOMY.md` already logs that broader claim as ambiguous, and this validation doesn't resolve it either, since it's still one product reviewed by a small number of people.
