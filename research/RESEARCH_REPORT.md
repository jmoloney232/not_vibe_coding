# Research Report: What Actually Makes an Interface Read as "Vibe Coded"

**Status of this document:** first-pass synthesis, written as findings arrived rather than after the fact. A methodology note up front, because it constrains everything below: this session's sandbox has no general network egress — only an allowlist (npm, pypi, anthropic, github). Direct attempts to reach production sites (stripe.com, etc.) returned a hard 403 at the connection layer, and `WebFetch` also failed on every real URL attempted (separate 403s, likely a proxy-side issue). That means this report cannot include fresh visual inspection of third-party production sites. Every claim below is one of exactly three kinds, and each is labeled:

- **[SEARCH]** — a claim or quote retrieved live via `WebSearch` this session, with a real, dated URL. This is real evidence, but it is search-snippet evidence — a summarized/excerpted view of a page, not a full fetched read of it.
- **[RECALLED]** — a description of a specific named product's visuals drawn from training knowledge, not from looking at it this session. Treated as lower-confidence than SEARCH, and flagged wherever used.
- **[OWN]** — evidence from this project's own rendered artifacts (the bike-shop and Perigee pages), which *were* actually screenshotted and inspected this session via local Playwright — this is the one category with full first-party visual verification.

This asymmetry is itself a finding worth stating plainly: a large fraction of "I looked at real websites and concluded X" work — by this agent or any agent operating under similar constraints — is more likely to be SEARCH/RECALLED-grade than the confident tone of a typical design write-up implies. Readers (and future agents extending this document) should weight conclusions accordingly, and should re-verify [RECALLED] claims by actually opening the cited products before leaning on them hard.

**Round 7 update:** a later session had real network access and did exactly that re-verification — see `research/INTERFACE_STUDY_RECORDS.md` for ten fully live-rendered, DOM/CSS-inspected records (tagged `[LIVE]`, a stronger tier than any evidence in this document). The headline result: this report's §1 negative-tracking-adjacent framing and `ANTI_VIBE_CODING_UI.md` §8's negative-letter-spacing AVOID entry did not survive live verification as stated — Stripe, Linear, Basecamp, and Varda all use tight negative tracking on their hero H1s, squarely in the range this document's framework flagged as a tell. Treat that specific claim as corrected, not merely re-confirmed; the rest of this report's [SEARCH]-grade claims were not all re-checked this round and should still be read with the caveat above until they are.

## 1. The central finding: pattern-avoidance and authorship are different axes

Every prior round of this project's own guide (`ANTI_VIBE_CODING_UI.md`) operated on an implicit model: vibe-coded-ness is a checklist of patterns (gradients, generic copy, shadcn defaults, negative tracking, opacity-fade hovers) and removing enough of them yields a good result. Two pieces of evidence this round directly contradict that model:

1. **[OWN]** After two rounds of pattern-removal on the Perigee page (fixing the button, fixing the tracking, then adding asymmetry and oversized type), a fresh blind reviewer — shown only the screenshot, no rationale — flagged the *newest* addition (corner-bracket framing around the orbit diagram) as "a common AI-tool-generated 'sci-fi HUD' motif" and rated the page's overall likelihood of being AI-generated as **medium confidence, not lower** than before the fix. The specific quote: *"the bracket-corner decorative elements around the diagram are a common AI-tool-generated 'sci-fi HUD' motif."* A second independent reviewer, given the same image with no framing, made the same call independently and predicted that a wider desktop viewport would look **more**, not less, generated, because "wider layouts give more room for the decorative bracket/HUD elements... to spread out, which tends to amplify that pitch-deck sci-fi feel." Both reviewers are reacting to a design decision that this project's own prior turn explicitly defended as "grounded in the Mission Control thesis" — i.e., the rationale existed, was internally consistent, and was wrong anyway. This is the single clearest piece of first-party evidence this round produced: **a plausible, internally-consistent design rationale is not evidence the design decision was good.** The task specification's warning that "a plausible explanation is not evidence of intentional design" is not a hypothetical risk here — it is exactly what happened one turn ago.

2. **[SEARCH]** Independent commentary on AI-generated interfaces converges on describing this same gap in *content* terms, not just visual-pattern terms: despite polished appearance, "something feels off—like talking to someone who's practiced being charming—with something hollow underneath" (dev.to, analysis of 100 vibe-coded sites). That "hollow" quality is not reducible to any single visual choice; it is a property of the *combination and motivation* behind choices — which matches finding #1 above almost exactly. The bracket motif wasn't hollow because brackets are bad; it was hollow because it was reached for as an available "technical-looking" decoration rather than because the page had real technical content to frame.

**Implication for the framework:** the old model ("remove clichéd patterns → get a good result") is necessarily incomplete, because pattern-removal is exactly the kind of activity that produces a new, different, equally-recognizable cliché (the "sci-fi HUD" decoration is *itself* now a recognized AI-generation tell, per the same reviewer). The revised framework needs a check that isn't "does this avoid known bad patterns" but "does this decoration exist because the content earned it, or because it was an available move." See Research Report §4 and the Realism/Authorship Audits added to the main guide.

## 2. Structural genericness: the page-architecture problem

**[SEARCH]** SaaS landing-page guidance is remarkably consistent, and remarkably explicit that the template *is* the industry-standard structure, not a corruption of it: "the core elements of the SaaS landing page template are a hero, social proof, problem, solution, FAQ, and CTA" (multiple sources, cross-referenced via unbounce.com, fibr.ai, orbix.studio). This is worth sitting with: the "hero → logos → features → testimonial → pricing → FAQ → CTA" shape that gets mocked as an AI tell predates AI page generation by years — it is simply what SaaS marketing pages have converged on because it converts. This means **structural conformity to this shape is not itself strong evidence of AI generation** — it's weak evidence at most, and the search results explicitly note that "success depends more on execution quality... rather than the structure itself." A framework that flags "has a hero, features, and a CTA" as a smell will produce false positives against a huge fraction of legitimately good, human-made marketing sites.

What *does* distinguish generic instances from good instances, per the same sources and the dashboard-criticism research below, is not the presence of the section but **whether each section's content could survive a product-name swap** — the Product Swap Test this project's guide already has (§20) turns out to be closer to the real mechanism than the structural-shape observation was. This is a case where the existing framework had the right test already and the wrong emphasis (treating "matches a known template shape" as the smell, rather than "content is interchangeable regardless of shape").

**[SEARCH]** The dashboard-specific version of the same finding: "one of the most common mistakes in dashboard design is displaying too many metrics at once, as when every piece of data appears equally important, users struggle to find meaningful insights" and "dashboards fail when designers treat them as data murals instead of tools... every element needs to earn its pixel space" (designrush.com, matillion.com, cross-referenced). Again, the failure is not "this is a dashboard" — it's "the components exist because the layout had room for them," which is the same underlying mechanism as the bracket-motif finding above, transposed from marketing pages to data interfaces.

## 3. Trend aggregation: individually-defensible, collectively-diagnostic

**[SEARCH]** Current (2026) design-trend commentary is unusually self-aware about this exact problem. One critique describes a trends presentation combining "neumorphism," "glassmorphism everywhere," and "pastel gradients" as "looking at what other designers think looks cool and calling it strategy," and states directly: "work that uses gradients, glass effects, or bento grids without thought, without purpose, and without any real sense of why, is bad" (creativeboom.com, studiomeyer.io). The emphasis in every source found is on **combination and intentionality**, not on any individual technique being disallowed — matching this project's own required framework addition (the Signal-Combination Model) almost exactly, and independently confirming that isolated-pattern bans were the wrong lever from the start.

**[SEARCH]** Corporate Memphis illustration is the most thoroughly documented case study of exactly this dynamic, and it maps unusually well onto "AI slop" discourse despite predating it by nearly a decade. The critique, consistently across sources (Creative Bloq, WIRED via Creative Bloq, AIGA Eye on Design, Webflow): the style is not disliked for its shapes in isolation but because "it strips away human individuality, creating a... visual language where everyone looks the same," it "feels like design without a real brief — something endlessly replicable," and because it depicts "a frictionless, joyful world" that reads as **generic positivity standing in for real product truth** — a direct precedent for this report's Realism Audit concerns (§11 below), from years before "vibe coding" was a term at all. This matters for calibration: the phenomenon this project is studying is not new or unique to AI-generated code. AI tools make it faster and cheaper to reach the same generic endpoint, but "endlessly replicable, built from basic shapes... without requiring actual [bespoke] work" describes a business incentive (cost-cutting, speed) that predates and outlives any specific generation method. A framework that treats "looks AI-generated" as a distinct category from "looks like a 2018 corporate-illustration template" is drawing a line the evidence doesn't support.

## 4. Realism and operational modeling: the strongest, most citable evidence found

This is the area with the most authoritative sourcing this round, via the completed background research on real-world friction and product specificity:

- **[SEARCH]** Nielsen Norman Group, a primary UX-authority source, states directly that placeholder/lorem-ipsum content "can derail usability testing sessions because placeholders provoke unintentional confusion," and that "designs with poor content fidelity are too abstract for users to understand" (nngroup.com/articles/promptframes). This is a documented-authority claim, not a critique-blog inference, and it applies with equal force to an AI agent populating a page with plausible-sounding placeholder metrics.
- **[SEARCH]** The FTC's 2024 rule banning fake/AI-generated consumer reviews and testimonials is a real regulatory fact, not a stylistic opinion — confirming the fake-social-proof pattern is common enough to draw legal action (Sidley Austin, Harvard Tagteam FTC summary).
- **[SEARCH]** Real vendor documentation of genuine operational edge cases — Kinde on billing proration math, Zuora on legacy/grandfathered plan lifecycle states, a Wix UX team case study where a simple permissions model had to be rebuilt into a full dedicated tab because "content alone" couldn't paper over real role complexity (wix-ux.com) — gives concrete, citable texture for what "real constraints" actually look like in production, as opposed to this project's own prior invented examples (a fictional bike shop's e-bike caveat, fictional orbital-manufacturing telemetry).
- **[SEARCH]** The clearest single-sentence articulation of the "themed vs. modeled" distinction, from marketing/demo-design critique: **"audiences judge believability through the content, not the chrome"** (marketmedianews.com) — directly naming the mechanism behind finding #1 in §1 above (a decorative chrome element cannot substitute for real content, no matter how well-motivated its rationale sounds).
- **Gap found, reported honestly:** the research specifically aimed at "cross-route incoherence" (polished homepage, generic app interior) turned up thin — no source was found naming this exact pattern directly, only adjacent front-stage/back-stage service-design theory and design-system-scaling critique. This is flagged rather than papered over with a confident-sounding synthesis; it remains a plausible mechanism (this project's own reasoning, not evidence) rather than a documented one.

## 5. Vocabulary and discourse: what the criticism actually targets

**[SEARCH]** Cross-referencing multiple 2026 sources on "AI slop" / "looks like AI made it" criticism: the recurring, specific visual complaints are narrower and more concrete than a vague "feels off" — sources repeatedly name **purple/blue gradients, Inter or system-default fonts, a four-card feature grid, and "safe spacing, predictable section order"** as the recognizable cluster (925studios.co, axe-web.com, medium.com/@sahilkargutkar.sk). One source's framing is worth quoting directly because it reframes the whole problem correctly: AI models trained on "thousands of successful SaaS websites" naturally "gravitate toward those patterns," producing what one source calls **"the mathematical average of the internet"** rather than a distinctive design (axe-web.com). That phrase — mathematical average — is a better operational definition of "vibe coded" than any single pattern-list, and is adopted in the revised guide's core definition below.

**[SEARCH]** The "shadcn-looking" discourse is unusually specific about mechanism: shadcn/ui is "explicitly designed to be copy-pasted by AI agents," which is *why* it has become a visual fingerprint of AI-assisted builds specifically, not because the components are poorly designed — "shadcn is not the problem—shadcn defaults are the problem" (Design Systems Collective, Medium; a Threads post titled "I love Shadcn except for that it looks like every other site using shadcn" captures the sentiment directly). The **"AI Purple Problem"** is the most explicitly generation-mechanism-aware term found: "observers have started mentally filtering out any AI landing page that uses [purple gradients]... registering it as 'generic AI sauce' before even reading a word," attributed to models that "generate the statistical average of their training data rather than making deliberate design choices" (Indie Hackers, prg.sh). This corroborates and sharpens the "mathematical average of the internet" framing from §5 above.

**Critically important counter-finding — people are not reliable AI-detectors, and this is directly documented, not assumed:**

- **The Monet incident (May 2026):** an artist posted a genuine, cropped 1915 Monet *Water Lilies* on X, labeled it AI-generated, and asked people to critique its flaws. Roughly 6.7 million views produced confident, detailed condemnations — "incoherent muddle of inconsistently saturated greens," an 850-word teardown citing "egregiously vague" background and lack of "cohesion to the depth and color choices" — all describing genuine Impressionist technique. Commentary on the episode: "People saw the 'Made with AI' label before they saw the painting, and their judgement was irreversibly clouded from the off" (Fortune, Creative Bloq, Futurism). This is the single best piece of evidence in this whole research pass that **stated or assumed provenance drives the "looks AI" judgment more than actual visual analysis does** — a direct empirical demonstration of exactly the risk the task brief warned about.
- **"AI slop" as social gatekeeping rather than detection:** multiple outlets document real human artists being falsely accused or banned (one painter banned from Reddit's r/Art for looking "too AI-like"), with academic framing noting accusations arise "through genuine error, stylistic conflation, or malice" rather than "empirically accurate detection" (Unite.AI, Forbes).
- **Adjacent, quantified precedent:** Turnitin's AI-text-detector false-positive rate (roughly 1-in-100 genuinely human-written submissions flagged) led Vanderbilt to disable it after finding it disproportionately misflagged non-native-English speakers — not a design-visual finding, but a well-sourced precedent that confident-sounding surface-feature detection of "AI origin" is measurably unreliable in an adjacent domain (certifiedimpact.com).

This project's revised framework needs to preserve this distinction sharply: "looks generic/templated" is a real, measurable, agreement-worthy perception; "was generated by AI" is a separate, much less reliable claim, and the two get conflated constantly in the discourse this section surveyed. See the Uncertainty Requirement added to the main guide.

## 6. Positive counter-calibration: restraint that works, decoration that fails

Two clean, well-documented reference points anchor the "Evaluation A vs. Evaluation B" split (does it look generated vs. is it a good interface) that this report's own framework revision leans on heavily:

- **[SEARCH]** GOV.UK's design system won the Design Museum's Design of the Year award specifically for "well thought out, yet understated design," built on a stated principle of "putting user need and convenience, not the needs of government, at its heart" (gds.blog.gov.uk, designsystems.one). This is restraint that reads as authored, not generic — because the restraint is instrumented to a specific, stated, verifiable purpose (citizens completing government tasks under legal/urgency pressure), not applied as a defensive "avoid AI clichés" posture.
- **[SEARCH]** Craigslist is the standard citation for the opposite pairing: a site widely agreed to be "downright ugly" by conventional visual standards, whose success is attributed directly to "subvert[ing] the Aesthetic-Usability Effect" — i.e., a case where Evaluation B (is it a good interface: yes, extremely, for two decades) is completely decoupled from Evaluation A in the visually-polished direction (vanseodesign.com, Pratt IXD design critique). Craigslist is not a counter-example to this project's guide; it is close to a positive example of Priority Tier ordering done correctly for its specific context (a high-frequency, text-dense, trust-through-familiarity marketplace where visual investment would plausibly cost more usability than it added).

The GOV.UK/Craigslist pairing is used directly in the revised framework's redefinition of "restraint" — distinguishing purposeful restraint (both cases) from defensive restraint (removing decoration specifically to avoid a "vibe coded" accusation, with no independent product reason), which is the actual failure mode Governing Principle 4's blandness note was gesturing at but didn't name precisely enough.

## 6a. Composition, tension, and rhythm: the actual mechanism, named precisely

**[SEARCH]** The single most load-bearing citation found this round, because it names a *mechanism* rather than a style: grid-breaking reads as intentional **only when the underlying structure is legible first** — "you must first establish the rule for its violation to register as a choice rather than an error" — and even then, restraint governs it: *"Restraint is the key word. Use it once and it's a statement. Use it four times and it's noise"* (Noble Intent, "Six Rule-Breaking Websites That Defy The Design Grid"). This reframes the prior round's asymmetric-hero fix correctly: an asymmetric grid only reads as deliberate if a viewer can sense the symmetric alternative it's deviating from — which is a testable, falsifiable claim, not a vibe.

**[SEARCH]** Vanseo Design's direct framing of tension: it is produced "through the asymmetrical balance of elements along with contrast of both tone and color," and "varying visual tension throughout a design by creating contrasts in space... creates rhythm and flow" — i.e., tension and rhythm are the same underlying mechanism (uneven contrast/spacing), not two separate techniques. This gives the revised guide's Composition Audit its actual operational test: not "is this asymmetric" but "does contrast/spacing vary meaningfully across the page, or is every region equally loud/quiet."

**[SEARCH]** A useful, moderately-confident explanation for *why* token-driven systems trend toward sameness: multiple sources converge that Tailwind's spacing scale is essentially "the Swiss grid restated in CSS classes" — i.e., token systems formalize the *rhythm/grid* layer of a real design tradition (Swiss design) without formalizing the *editorial risk-taking* layer (asymmetric cropping, a single dominant typographic gesture, deliberate rule-breaking) that made Swiss design distinctive in the first place. This matters directly for this project's own habits (see Self-Audit): building a token system was treated, in every prior round, as evidence of rigor — this research suggests token systems are necessary but structurally incapable of producing the actual differentiator, because they only encode the part of the tradition that was never the distinctive part.

## 6b. Anti-AI minimalism: restraint that reads as soulless, with a very concrete recent case

**[SEARCH]** The clearest single articulation of "restraint vs. blandness" found this round: modern minimalist products "look like a clone of the next — slick, sanitized, and strangely soulless," and the cause is minimalism "stripped of intention," where "the elements that remain aren't doing any work" (Medium/Bootcamp, Alexis Collins). This is the precise failure mode Governing Principle 4's blandness note gestures at but doesn't name mechanically — the revised guide operationalizes it as: for every remaining element after a restraint pass, can you state what work it does, or did it simply survive because removing it wasn't flagged by a rule?

**[SEARCH] — the strongest, freshest, most concrete evidence in this entire research pass:** the Cracker Barrel 2025 logo/rebrand backlash. A real, dated, financially-consequential case: within 48 hours of the minimalist rebrand's reveal, "terms like 'generic,' 'soulless,' and 'bland' were trending," commentators named a "'blandification' epidemic," and the stock dropped roughly 12-14% (~$100M market cap). A follow-up report **dated 2026-07-28 — two days before this research was conducted** — states the CEO stepped down about a year later, explicitly tied to the incident, quoted saying "I feel like I've been fired by America" (CNBC, Creative Bloq, 24/7 Wall St.). This is not a web-interface case, but it is the single most concrete, dated, real-stakes demonstration available that removing distinctive character in the name of clean/modern/safe design is not a neutral or automatically-safe move — it has a measured cost, recently, in public. It is used directly in the revised guide as the canonical example for why "defensive restraint" (removing a brand's distinctive signature specifically to look safe/modern/inoffensive) is treated as a real failure mode with real consequences, not just a theoretical risk.

## 7. What this means for the existing guide (summary; full diff in FRAMEWORK_DIFF.md)

The research converges on six specific corrections to the prior model, each detailed with mechanism in the revised `ANTI_VIBE_CODING_UI.md`:

1. **Pattern-avoidance is not authorship.** A design choice motivated by "this avoids a known AI cliché" is exactly as unexamined as one motivated by "this is what the library ships." Both are choices made by rule-following rather than by product reasoning, and both can produce a new cliché (see §1's bracket-motif finding).
2. **Structural shape (hero/features/CTA; dashboard/cards/chart) is weak evidence on its own.** It predates AI tooling, converts for real reasons, and the actual test is content interchangeability (Product Swap Test), not shape presence.
3. **Realism/content fidelity is a stronger, more citable signal cluster than visual-pattern presence.** NN/g, the FTC, and real vendor edge-case documentation all point the same direction: fabricated-feeling content (placeholder metrics, generic testimonials, decoration without informational payload) is the more diagnostic and more authoritative signal than radius or gradient choices.
4. **Restraint and decoration are each bimodal, not unimodal.** Both can be purposeful (GOV.UK; a justified brand signature) or defensive/hollow (a "sci-fi HUD" bracket reached for because it was available; minimalism adopted specifically to dodge criticism — the Cracker Barrel case shows this has real, measured cost, not just theoretical risk). The old guide had asymmetric machinery for this — lots of structure for catching unjustified decoration, much less for catching defensive/hollow restraint — and the revision corrects that imbalance.
5. **Grid-breaking/asymmetry only registers as deliberate if the rule it breaks is legible first, and only if used sparingly.** "Use it once and it's a statement, use it four times and it's noise" (Noble Intent). This gives the Composition Audit an actual test instead of a vibe: can a viewer sense the more conventional alternative this choice deviates from, and is the deviation rare enough to still register as a choice.
6. **Token/spacing systems encode only the rhythm layer of real design traditions, not the risk-taking layer.** Tailwind's spacing scale is, per multiple sources, close to Swiss design's grid formalized in CSS — but Swiss design's distinctiveness came from the editorial risk-taking layered on top of that grid, which no token system can generate by itself. This directly corrects this project's own recurring habit (see Self-Audit) of treating "I built a token system" as evidence of rigor or authorship — it is neither; it's necessary infrastructure that has never, on its own, been the differentiator.
