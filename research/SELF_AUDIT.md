# Self-Audit: Blind Review of This Project's Own Output

## Method

Two prior outputs from this project were reviewed: the bike-shop example (`example.html`, round 3) and the Perigee landing page (`landing.html`, current/round-5 state, after the asymmetry/composition pass). Both were actually rendered and screenshotted via local Playwright this session — this is real, first-party visual evidence, not a description from memory.

Two independent fresh subagents reviewed the screenshots with **no shared context**: no mention this was "my" work, no mention of the anti-vibe-coding guide, no rationale, no framing about what the pages were "supposed" to achieve. Neutral prompts only ("here is a screenshot of a real website, give your honest first impression"). Their full, unedited findings are in `INDEPENDENT_REVIEWS.md`. This document is the qualitative critique built on top of those findings, plus the recurring-habit analysis the task requires — written after reading the blind reviews, not before, per Part 5's instruction not to re-read prior rationale first.

## Bike-shop example: qualitative critique

**What appears to have generated this / what era does it resemble:** A restrained, text-first local-business informational page. Reviewer 1's unprompted read: "Utilitarian, like a printed index card turned into a webpage." Nothing about it pattern-matches a specific AI-tool visual signature (no gradient, no card grid, no purple) — if anything, its visual bareness works *against* the common "AI slop" cluster (Tier 7 in `TAXONOMY.md`), which depends on decoration, not absence of it.

**Which patterns feel default vs. authored:** The layout (header bar + CTA, alternating price rows, muted terracotta accent) was independently named "a very common 'local service business' template pattern" by Reviewer 1 — i.e., the *shape* reads as templated even though nothing about the shape is AI-specific; this is Tier 2 (structural signal) doing exactly the weak-evidence-alone work `TAXONOMY.md` predicts. What reads as authored, per both reviewers, is entirely in the copy: "Sam Ostrander and one part-time mechanic," the e-bike caveat, the loading-zone note. This matches the research finding almost exactly: content carries more of the authorship signal than layout does.

**What's memorable / what feels fake:** Reviewer 1: "the specific, slightly grumpy operational rules... that's the one thing with personality." Also, unprompted and independently, flagged the bolded "today" row in the hours table as "slightly uncanny/systematic, like a template auto-bolding whatever day it is rather than a human choice" — a genuinely new finding this project's own three prior review passes (round 3's build, its self-critique, and the round-3 Appendix F write-up) never caught. That's a real miss: a mechanically-correct feature (today's row bolds based on real computed date) reads, to a fresh eye, as evidence of automation rather than care, exactly backwards from its actual purpose.

**Product-specific vs. cosmetically customized:** The name, address, and prices are swappable window dressing (Tier 3's "themed" half); the specific operational caveats (e-bike/cargo-bike exclusion, walk-in cutoff time) are the "modeled" half and are real per Reviewer 1's assessment. Both reviewers, independently, converged on the same single fix: **add a real photo.** Neither was asked about imagery specifically — both volunteered it as the first thing they'd change, because the page currently has zero evidence the business physically exists.

**Does the design contain meaningful tension:** No, deliberately — and per `TAXONOMY.md` Tier 8, that's a legitimate choice for this product category if the plainness is instrumented to a purpose (a phone-checking, in-a-hurry visitor). This project's round-3 self-assessment claimed exactly this justification at the time. The blind review doesn't contradict that framing, but it also didn't independently arrive at it — it read the plainness as "bare" more than "purposefully restrained," which is a real ambiguity `TAXONOMY.md`'s Tier 8 flags as genuinely hard to resolve from inspection alone (you often need to know *why* restraint was chosen, not just observe that it was).

**Verdict:** Held up reasonably on content/authorship grounds (Tier 3/4), which corroborates the round-3 Appendix F assessment. **New finding this round didn't have before:** the "today" auto-bold detail is a small but real instance of a mechanically-correct feature undermining its own authorship signal — logged as a recurring-habit candidate below (habit: features that are functionally correct but read as automated because there's no human-legible reason shown for the behavior).

## Perigee landing page: qualitative critique

**What appears to have generated this / what era does it resemble:** Reviewer 1's unprompted read: "This is the exact dark-mode hard-tech template... this exact visual vocabulary has become its own genre in the last few years." This is a direct, blind confirmation that the page pattern-matches a *specific, nameable, already-generic* visual genre (dark background, single amber/orange accent, monospace all-caps labels, numbered 01-02-03-04 steps, corner-bracket framing) — not a neutral "clean, modern" read, a "this is a known type" read.

**Which decisions feel authored vs. inherited:** Both reviewers, independently, singled out the exact same detail as the one genuinely credible, authored element on the page: the "Illustrative telemetry — orbit accelerated ~600x for display" caption. Reviewer 1: "That caveat line is the single most credible, non-generic detail on the page." Reviewer 2, unprompted in a completely separate framing (Adversarial AI-Detection role): flagged the same caption as the thing that makes the diagram read as "a classic stand-in for content nobody actually built real footage for" — **the same sentence was read by one reviewer as the strongest evidence of authorship and by the other as reinforcing evidence of placeholder content.** This disagreement is recorded, not resolved, per the task's explicit instruction not to force consensus — see `INDEPENDENT_REVIEWS.md`.

**The most important finding — a rationale that didn't survive contact with a blind eye:** The previous turn's fix explicitly added corner-bracket framing around the orbit diagram, defended in that turn's own text as "grounded in the Mission Control creative thesis already established for this page." Reviewer 1, given zero context, called it "a very common 'sci-fi HUD' signifier" that makes the diagram "read as decoration wearing the costume of data." Reviewer 2, independently and without being asked about brackets specifically, converged on the same diagram as "a classic AI-tool-generated 'sci-fi HUD' motif" with medium confidence, and further predicted — again unprompted — that the desktop-width version (which neither reviewer was shown) would look **more**, not less, AI-generated, "since wider layouts give more room for the decorative bracket/HUD elements... to spread out." **This is the single most important finding in this entire self-audit.** A design decision was made, defended with an internally-consistent rationale tied to a stated creative thesis, and independently read by two blind reviewers as reinforcing exactly the failure it was trying to fix. The rationale was plausible. The rationale was wrong. This is the task brief's warning ("a plausible explanation is not evidence of intentional design") realized concretely, one turn before the warning was even issued.

**What's memorable after looking away:** Both reviewers named the same telemetry caption line, not any visual element — a second, independent confirmation that this page's most successful moment is a content decision (an honest disclosure), not a compositional one, despite two full rounds of compositional work (button/typography fix, then asymmetry/rhythm fix) being where all the design effort actually went.

**First thing an experienced designer would change:** Both reviewers converged on essentially the same fix, again independently: replace the illustrative/placeholder-feeling diagram with something that carries real information. Reviewer 1: "Replace the placeholder orbit diagram with an actual data visualization — a real chart of fiber defect density (ground vs. orbit)." This is a strong, convergent, actionable finding and is used directly in the redesign exercise below.

**Verdict:** The prior two rounds of fixes (button/typography, then composition/asymmetry) were real and defensible on their own narrow terms — they fixed exactly what they targeted. But neither round touched the actual load-bearing problem two independent blind reviewers found immediately: the page's one visual "signature" device carries almost no information relative to its prominence, and the fix for that is not more composition work, it's **replacing decoration with real content** (Tier 3/6 in `TAXONOMY.md`, not Tier 1/2). This is exactly the miscalibration the task brief predicted: work on visual patterns (composition, asymmetry, tension) while the actual gap was product/content modeling.

## Recurring generation habits identified (Part 9)

For each: evidence, why it likely occurs, effect, detection, correction.

### 1. Treating a defensible rationale as proof of a good decision
- **Evidence:** the bracket-motif finding above — an explicit, internally-consistent rationale ("grounded in the Mission Control thesis") was written and believed at the time, and was wrong.
- **Why it occurs:** rationale-writing happens in the same reasoning pass as the decision itself, so the rationale is optimized to be *consistent with* the decision already made, not to independently test it. This is confirmation, not verification.
- **Effect:** decisions that fail in exactly the way the guide's own Governing Principle 10 already named for source-code comments ("performative design reasoning") but hadn't yet caught happening in the *design decision itself*, only in comments describing it.
- **Detection:** before accepting a rationale, ask whether it was written before or after the decision, and whether an adversarial reviewer with no access to the rationale would reach the same conclusion. This project only found out the rationale was wrong by actually running that adversarial check — which it had not done in the same turn the decision was made.
- **Correction:** the Blind Review Requirement (added to the revised guide) makes this non-optional for any decision defended primarily by its stated thesis/rationale rather than by independent inspection.

### 2. Reaching for a "technical-looking" decorative device when a real data source would serve the same purpose better
- **Evidence:** the orbit diagram + corner brackets exist to signal "this is a technical, data-driven product," but both blind reviewers pointed out the diagram itself contains almost no real information (an ellipse, two dots, one caption sentence).
- **Why it occurs:** a decorative device that gestures at a category (technical/HUD/mission-control) is much cheaper to produce than actually sourcing or modeling real domain data, and it satisfies a surface-level check ("does this look technical") without satisfying the deeper one ("is there real technical content here").
- **Effect:** exactly the Tier 7 "hollow decoration" cluster named in `TAXONOMY.md` — a stated thesis plus near-zero information behind the device it produced.
- **Detection:** for any element justified as "reinforcing the thesis" or "on-brand," ask what specific information it conveys, and whether that information is real or illustrative-as-a-stand-in. If it's a stand-in, ask why real information wasn't used instead of a placeholder wearing the visual costume of data.
- **Correction:** the Realism Audit (added to the revised guide) makes "does this decorative element carry real information proportional to its visual weight" an explicit, required question, not an implicit hope.

### 3. Doing composition work when the actual gap was content/product modeling
- **Evidence:** two full rounds of effort (button/type fix; then asymmetry/rhythm fix) were both compositional, and neither was what either blind reviewer identified as the page's real weakness (an under-informative signature visual).
- **Why it occurs:** composition work is bounded, checkable, and has a clear "before/after" the agent can verify itself (screenshot comparison) — whereas "does this page contain real domain modeling" has no equivalent quick self-check, so it's easier to keep iterating on the axis that's easy to verify.
- **Effect:** genuine visual improvement (this round's asymmetric-hero pass measurably reduced some generic tells) that nonetheless left the user's core complaint — "this looks vibe coded" — essentially unaddressed, because the complaint was never actually about composition specifically.
- **Detection:** before starting a fix pass, classify the suspected problem against the taxonomy tiers *first* — if the likely issue is Tier 3/6 (product/content modeling), compositional work on Tier 1/2 should not be the first move, even though it's more tractable.
- **Correction:** the revised guide's Definition of Done now requires stating which taxonomy tier a fix targets before making it, specifically to prevent defaulting to the easiest-to-verify axis.

### 4. Same underlying mental move across different visual styles
- **Evidence:** the bike-shop page (plain, restrained) and the Perigee page (dark, technical, decorated) look opposite, but both blind reviews converged on the same underlying finding for each: the page's most credible/authored moment is a content decision (an operational caveat; an honest telemetry disclosure), and its weakest moment is a visual default (a templated local-business shape; a hollow HUD motif) reached for without being individually reconsidered.
- **Why it occurs:** this project's process (per its own §17 Implementation Workflow) front-loads visual-system decisions (tokens, type scale, color roles) before, or in parallel with, real content modeling — so content ends up being the one thing actually written fresh for the specific product each time, while layout/decoration draws from a smaller mental library of "moves that read as considered" (asymmetry, monospace labels, corner brackets, editorial two-column rails) that gets reapplied across genuinely different products.
- **Detection:** ask, for the current build, whether the same 3-4 compositional moves would have been reached for regardless of what this specific product was. If yes, they're library moves, not product-derived ones — even if they're not literally from a component library.
- **Correction:** the Authorship Audit (added to the revised guide) asks explicitly "which decisions would be difficult to reproduce by swapping the product's text" — applied to composition, not just content, going forward.

### 5. Self-review that checks compliance with the guide's own rules rather than checking the visual outcome
- **Evidence:** every prior round's self-critique (§19 in the old guide) asked whether specific named rules were followed (contrast computed? tokens unused? reduced-motion respected?) — all real, all correctly verified via Playwright — but none of that checklist would have caught the bracket-motif problem, because "does this look like a sci-fi HUD cliché" was never one of the rules being checked against.
- **Why it occurs:** compliance checks are boundable and automatable (grep for unused tokens, run a contrast formula, screenshot at four widths); "does this look distinctive to a stranger" is not, so the checklist naturally accretes items that are checkable and under-represents the one thing that actually needs a fresh, unbiased eye.
- **Effect:** high confidence in a build ("Playwright suite passed, contrast verified, focus-visible confirmed") that is entirely orthogonal to whether the build still reads as generic — exactly what happened at the end of the previous turn, immediately before this deep-research request.
- **Detection:** track, per build, how much of the "Verification Completed" list is mechanical/automatable versus how much required an actual independent eye. If it's ~100% the former, the build has not actually been checked for the thing this whole guide exists to catch.
- **Correction:** the Blind Review Requirement and the First-Impression Review (both added to the revised guide) are now load-bearing completion gates, not optional appendices — a build cannot be marked done on compliance-checklist grounds alone.
